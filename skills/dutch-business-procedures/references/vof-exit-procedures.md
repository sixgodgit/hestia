# VOF Exit Procedures — Detailed Guide

## Scenario
A partner in a Dutch VOF (general partnership, typically a restaurant or small business) wants to exit. The other partner is uncooperative — won't sign dissolution papers, won't pay the remaining buyout amount.

## Legal Framework

### Art. 7A:1683 BW — Unilateral dissolution
> "Een vof wordt ontbonden door opzegging van een vennoot aan de andere vennoten."

Key points:
- One partner can unilaterally dissolve the VOF by notifying the others
- No specific form required (vormvrij) — letter, email, or even conduct suffices
- Recommended to include an opzegtermijn (notice period) to respect partners' legitimate interests
- Other partners can only oppose if opzegging violates "redelijkheid en billijkheid" (Art. 7A:1686 lid 1 BW)

### Art. 7A:1684 BW jo Art. 16 WvK — Court dissolution
If partners disagree about dissolution, a partner can apply to the court (rechtbank) for dissolution based on "gewichtige redenen" (weighty reasons):
- Permanently disturbed relationship (verstoorde verhouding)
- Malpractice by a partner
- Fundamental disagreement on business direction

### Art. 32 WvK — Liquidation
After dissolution, assets must be liquidated: ongoing business settled, debts paid, remaining assets divided.

## Step-by-Step Exit Procedure

### Step 1: Send opzeggingsbrief (termination notice)

**Method:** Aangetekende brief (registered mail via PostNL) — costs ~€12, provides legal proof of delivery.

**Content should include:**
- Clear statement of intent to dissolve the VOF
- VOF name and KVK number
- Date of dissolution (end of notice period)
- Grounds for dissolution (optional but recommended)
- Demand for payment of remaining amount + deadline (14 days typical)
- Bank account (IBAN) for payment
- Request for cooperation with KVK Form 17
- Warning of legal action if no compliance

**Language:** Dutch (legally binding). Provide Chinese translation for reference.

### Step 2: Self-deregister from KVK (unilateral)

**Portal:** Mijn KVK (kvk.nl/mijnkvk) with DigiD login

The exiting partner can remove themselves as functionaris/vennoot via Form 16 — **no other partner's signature required.**

This immediately:
- Removes their name from the Handelsregister (public record)
- Stops their liability for NEW debts of the VOF
- Does NOT eliminate liability for debts incurred during their membership

### Step 3: Request Form 17 (full dissolution)

After self-deregistration, request the remaining partner to sign Form 17 to fully dissolve the VOF. The remaining partner must then register a new business form (eenmanszaak or BV) if they want to continue operating.

### Step 4: If remaining partner refuses

**Option A — Negotiation:** Hire a Dutch lawyer (advocaat) specializing in ondernemingsrecht (commercial law) to send a formal letter. Often resolves the issue.

**Option B — Court dissolution:** File a verzoekschrift (petition) at the local rechtbank for ontbinding based on gewichtige redenen. Costs: €3,000-10,000. Timeline: 3-6 months (or 2-4 weeks for kort geding / expedited procedure).

**Option C — Simultaneously pursue debt recovery:** If there's an agreed buyout amount that's unpaid, this is a separate civil claim (vordering tot betaling). Can be combined with dissolution or pursued independently.

## Financial Settlement

### What the exiting partner is entitled to:
- Return of capital contribution (minus any agreed deductions)
- Share of accumulated profits (if any)
- Share of asset appreciation

### Common disputes:
- **Valuation of the business** — often the crux. Without a partnership agreement specifying valuation method, parties may need an independent business valuation (bedrijfswaardering).
- **Set-off against debts** — the VOF may claim the exiting partner owes money (e.g., share of losses). Must be proven.
- **Payment timeline** — negotiate installment plan if lump sum is impossible.

### Tax implications:
- Stakingswinst (cessation profit) may be taxable
- Final BTW (VAT) and IB (income tax) returns required
- Consult a Dutch tax advisor (belastingadviseur) or accountant

## Practical Tips

1. **Document everything** — all communications, agreements, payments received
2. **Act promptly** — the longer you stay registered, the more liability you accumulate
3. **Don't abandon the process** — if the other partner ghosts you, escalate to a lawyer
4. **Check for personal guarantees** — if you personally guaranteed any VOF debts (bank loans, supplier credit), exiting the VOF doesn't release those guarantees
5. **Notify creditors directly** — for full liability protection, known creditors should be individually notified of your exit (Art. 13 WvK)

## Key Differences: VOF vs BV exit

| Aspect | VOF | BV |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Exit mechanism | Unilateral opzegging (Art. 7A:1683 BW) | Share transfer or BV dissolution |
| Requires court? | No (unless disputed) | Only for dissolution |
| Notaris needed? | No | Yes (for BV dissolution) |
| Partner liability | Joint & several, unlimited | Limited to shares |
| Public registration | KVK Handelsregister | KVK Handelsregister |
